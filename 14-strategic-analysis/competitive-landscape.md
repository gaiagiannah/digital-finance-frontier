# Competitive Landscape

## Settlement Layer

| Player | Strength | Weakness | 2030 Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visa** | 130+ countries; 9 chains; $7B run rate; VSP | Not a ledger owner; dependent on partners | Dominant stablecoin settlement layer |
| **Mastercard** | 150M merchants; BVNK; $1T target; 100+ partners | Newer to crypto; integration complexity | #2 stablecoin settlement; strong in APAC |
| **SWIFT** | 11,000+ institutions; 200+ countries; ISO 20022 | Legacy; not atomic; defensive posture | One option among many; not default |
| **Canton** | Privacy; institutional trust; G7 standard | Permissioned; limited to G7; Daml ecosystem | Institutional tokenized deposit standard (G7) |
| **TCH** | US banks; Fed relationship; 16+ participants | US-only; not yet live (H1 2027) | US tokenized deposit standard |
| **Agorá** | Central bank backing; atomic; 8 currencies | Complex; multi-party governance; slow | Wholesale cross-border settlement standard |
| **mBridge** | State backing; $55B proven; 98% cost reduction | China-centric; sanctions risk; closed | China/Gulf corridor standard |
| **Kinexys** | JPMorgan scale; $900B repo; live | Proprietary; not open; JPMorgan-only | JPMorgan's internal standard |

### Settlement Layer: Who Wins?

**No single winner.** The end-state is **multiple coexisting rails**:
- **Visa/Mastercard**: Retail + mid-market stablecoin payments
- **Canton/TCH**: Institutional tokenized deposits (G7)
- **Agorá**: Wholesale cross-border (central bank money)
- **mBridge**: China/Gulf corridor
- **SWIFT**: Legacy + bridge (when correspondent banking required)
- **Public chains (Base, Ethereum)**: DeFi + open tokenized assets

## Tokenization Layer

| Player | Strength | Weakness | 2030 Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BlackRock** | $15B+ BUIDL; brand; distribution; DTCC pilot | Concentration risk; US-centric | #1 tokenized asset issuer |
| **JPMorgan** | Kinexys + Onyx; $900B repo; institutional trust | Proprietary; not open | #1 institutional tokenization platform |
| **DTCC** | Systemic importance; regulatory trust; 50+ firms | Slow; legacy infrastructure; US-only | US tokenized securities authority |
| **Ondo** | Multi-chain; institutional flow; open | Smaller; less brand; less distribution | #1 open RWA protocol |
| **Kraken xStocks** | Nasdaq integration; open; institutional | Smaller; newer; less brand | Tokenized equity platform (Nasdaq) |
| **Franklin Templeton** | BENJI; DBS collateral acceptance | Smaller; less diverse | Tokenized MMF leader |
| **NYSE** | 24/7 trading; T+0; regulatory approval | New; execution risk | Tokenized equity exchange |

### Tokenization Layer: Key Dynamics

1. **DTCC + NYSE** = US tokenized equities standard (Oct 2026)
2. **BlackRock + Ondo** = Tokenized treasuries/credit standard
3. **JPMorgan Kinexys** = Institutional payments standard (proprietary)
4. **Canton + TCH** = Tokenized deposit standard (G7)
5. **Kraken xStocks + Nasdaq** = Alternative tokenized equity platform

## Distribution Layer

| Player | Strength | Weakness | 2030 Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coinbase** | 110M+ users; Base L2; AgentKit; US listing | Regulatory (US); competition from incumbents | #1 crypto-native distribution; Base = institutional L2 |
| **Schwab** | 40M+ accounts; 75 bps; brand trust | Skeptical of tokenization; slow | #1 retail crypto (via existing accounts) |
| **Fidelity** | 30M+ accounts; stablecoin; NYSE; FCAT | Slower to innovate; traditional | #2 retail crypto; #1 tokenized fund |
| **Vanguard** | $12.5T AUM; brand trust | Still in evaluation; no product yet | #3 (if they move); potential game-changer |
| **PayPal** | 400M+ users; PYUSD; merchant network | Crypto is add-on, not core | Stablecoin payment distribution |
| **MetaMask** | 50M+ users; self-custody; DeFi access | No institutional focus; self-custody risk | #1 self-custody wallet; DeFi on-ramp |

### Distribution Layer: Key Dynamics

1. **Schwab + Fidelity + Vanguard** = Traditional distribution (40M+ 30M+ 12.5T)
2. **Coinbase** = Crypto-native distribution (110M+)
3. **PayPal** = Payment distribution (400M+)
4. **The question**: Do traditional brokers (Schwab, Fidelity, Vanguard) become the primary distribution channel for tokenized assets? Or does Coinbase/Base capture it?
5. **Most likely**: Both. Traditional brokers for tokenized funds/equities; Coinbase for crypto-native + DeFi.

## Intelligence / Forensics Layer

| Player | Strength | Weakness | 2030 Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chainalysis** | 6,500+ VASPs; 100+ LE; court record; Kryptos | Less transparent; vendor-hosted | #1 law enforcement tool; court standard |
| **TRM Labs** | FedRAMP High; glass-box; federal focus | Less visual; smaller VASP DB | #1 federal/government tool |
| **Elliptic** | Cross-chain (99%); EU/FCA; DeFi depth | Less LE focus; UK-centric | #1 EU + cross-chain tool |
| **Inca Digital** | Multi-source (dark web + social + blockchain + HUMINT) | Smaller; niche | #1 multi-source intelligence |
| **Kroll** | Traditional + blockchain hybrid; global; restructuring | Less crypto-specific | #1 professional services (crypto) |
| **Crystal** | Sanctions; Eastern Europe; on-prem | Niche | #1 sanctions compliance |
| **Allium** | Raw data; custom models; on-prem | No investigation UI | #1 data infrastructure |

### Intelligence Layer: Key Dynamics

1. **Chainalysis** = Law enforcement standard (court-admissible)
2. **TRM Labs** = Federal government standard (FedRAMP)
3. **Elliptic** = EU + cross-chain standard
4. **Inca Digital** = Multi-source intelligence (dark web + social + blockchain)
5. **Kroll** = Professional services (litigation, restructuring, due diligence)
6. **The gap**: No unified multi-chain, multi-jurisdiction, multi-source platform exists. This is the opportunity.
